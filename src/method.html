Equations & limits

This reference describes the equations and calculation sequence implemented in Joule3D. All solves run in a browser Web Worker. Model calculates on page load and 0.8 seconds after the last input change. Screening remains an explicit batch run. A Cartesian finite-volume discretization resolves electrical potential and temperature in the heater and electrodes, velocity, pressure and temperature in the gas, and temperature in the selected reactor wall. Geometry is represented by occupied grid cells. The application uses its own numerical solver; it does not run FEniCS or COMSOL.

X and Y are transverse coordinates; Z is the axial coordinate. The origin is the center of the computational bounding box. Input lengths are in mm and input temperatures in °C; internal calculations use m and K. Temperature differences have the same numerical value in K and °C. Electrical resistivity ρe [Ω m] and mass density ρm [kg/m³] are independent inputs. The gas pressure field is gauge pressure relative to the outlet, in Pa.

Δx = W/Nx, Δy = H/Ny, Δz = L/Nz
Vc = Δx Δy Δz
Ax = Δy Δz, Ay = Δx Δz, Az = Δx Δy

The default grid has N × N × 3N cells. Each directional count can also be specified independently. The ordinary limits are 96 cells per axis and 60,000 cells in the bounding box. For thin rectangular heaters with gas enabled and no volumetric wall, automatic Y resolution may reach 512 cells. It retains at least three cells across thickness with represented thickness within 2%. If the cell budget requires it, automatic X and Z counts are reduced while retaining at least three cells across heater width (within 2%) and 18 axial cells. Explicit independent counts are honored or rejected when they exceed the budget. Physical heater and channel dimensions remain unchanged. This geometric criterion does not establish mesh convergence. Cells outside the electrical solid are excluded from its potential solve. CSG union and subtraction operations are applied in input order. STL import checks closed-edge connectivity and uses ray intersections to classify the interior. Imported STL geometry is recentered and scaled independently along each axis to the edited bounding dimensions. Reducing the bounding box can clip CSG primitives.

A hollow tube contains cells satisfying ID/2 ≤ √(x²+y²) ≤ OD/2. To retain a connected annulus and an open bore, the transverse grid is refined until Δx, Δy ≤ min(t/1.5, ID/3), where t = (OD−ID)/2. The bore criterion is omitted at ID = 0. This is a minimum geometric resolution criterion; temperature and pressure-drop convergence still require a mesh study. If the required grid exceeds a limit, the solve fails without changing the physical dimensions.

Finite electrodes occupy a specified length at each end of the solid. The central heater length is approximately L−2Lelectrode. Interfaces lie on cell faces, so the represented length can differ from the input by about one cell. The entire electrical solid must form one connected component. Floating conductors and independent electrical circuits are unsupported.

2. Potential, resistance and Joule heating

2.1 Governing equations and electrical boundaries

∇·J = 0
J = −σ(T)∇φ,   σ(T) = 1/ρe(T)
qJ = J·E = σ|∇φ|²,   E = −∇φ

Electrical equilibrium is treated as instantaneous relative to thermal evolution. Charge storage, displacement current, inductance and AC skin effects are omitted. Terminal A lies on the occupied minimum-Z end face; terminal B lies on the maximum-Z end face. Only faces within the specified contact width and X offset receive a terminal voltage. All other electrical surfaces satisfy J·n = 0. With finite electrodes enabled, ideal terminals act only at the outer end faces; voltage drops within the electrodes are resolved.

2.2 Face conductance and contact resistance

For adjacent cells a and b separated by Δℓ across face area Af, the two half-cell resistances are connected in series. Electrical contact resistivity r″c [Ω m²] adds resistance at a finite-electrode/heater interface.

Ra,f = ρe,a Δℓ/(2Af)
Rb,f = ρe,b Δℓ/(2Af)
Rc,f = r″c/Af
ge,f = 1/(Ra,f + Rb,f + Rc,f)
Iab = ge,f(φa−φb)
Σb ge,ab(φa−φb) + Σterminal ge,at(φa−φt) = 0

In ideal-terminal mode, the terminal-to-cell connection includes one half-cell resistance and r″c/A. With finite electrodes, contact resistance moves to the internal electrode/heater interface and is not added again at the outer terminal. Reactor walls remain outside the electrical domain, including when a metallic wall preset is selected. Direct wall/heater contact is electrically insulated.

2.3 Unit-voltage solve and terminal resistance

ψA = 1, ψB = 0
G = ΣA faces ge,at(1−ψa),   R = 1/G
φa = V ψa,   I = GV,   P = GV²

A 1 V potential field is solved at the current temperature. Its conductance G [S] determines the actual voltage allowed by the supply, after which potential and current are scaled. Temperature-dependent resistivity causes the unit-potential problem to be solved again during thermal iterations. The reported terminal resistance includes the current temperature, grid, electrode placement and electrical contact resistance.

2.4 Conservative allocation of Joule heat

Pf = ge,f(φa−φb)² = Iab²(Ra,f+Rb,f+Rc,f)
Pa←f = Iab²(Ra,f + Rc,f/2)
Pb←f = Iab²(Rb,f + Rc,f/2)
qJ,a = ΣPa←f/Vc

Internal contact heat is divided equally between the two adjacent cells. Heat generated at an ideal terminal contact is deposited entirely in the adjacent solid cell. The separately reported Contact heat is already included in total terminal power P and must not be added as another heat source. Displayed current density J is reconstructed by averaging face current densities into a cell vector. Heat generation uses the conservative face-resistance allocation above, rather than the square of that reconstructed vector.

3. Power supply: CV, CC, CP, caps and pulses

The supply setpoint is in V, A or W according to the selected control mode. As resistance changes with temperature, actual voltage, current and power change within the supply envelope.

Vreq = Vset   (CV)
Vreq = Iset/G   (CC)
Vreq = √(Pset/G)   (CP)
Vcap = min[Vreq, Vmax, Imax/G, √(Pmax/G)]
0 ≤ V ≤ Vmax,   0 ≤ I ≤ Imax,   VI ≤ Pmax

Clamp mode selects the most restrictive voltage, current or power bound. The feasible V–I region is bounded by Vmax, Imax and I = Pmax/V. Trip mode aborts the calculation when an electrical evaluation requests a voltage beyond a bound. Protection also applies during nonlinear iterations. The code does not locate the exact physical time at which a protection threshold is crossed.

Vslew = max[Vold−sΔt, min(Vold+sΔt, Vcap)]
V = min[Vslew, Vmax, Imax/G, √(Pmax/G)]

The voltage slew rate s is in V/s. Setting it to zero gives an ideal instantaneous response. Slew applies only to transient studies, starting from zero voltage. Voltage, current and power caps take precedence over slew, so voltage may fall faster than the specified rate when a cap requires it. During pulse-off periods, residual voltage can produce power while ramping down. Inductors, capacitors and the internal control dynamics of an actual power supply are omitted.

The J trip condition checks the maximum reconstructed cell current density. It cannot resolve continuum singularities at contact edges or local peaks smaller than a cell.

4. Solid heat transfer and temperature-dependent properties

ρm ∂hm(T)/∂t = ∇·[k(T)∇T] + qJ
dhm/dT = Cp(T)
Kab = Af / [Δℓ/(2ka) + Δℓ/(2kb) + r″th]
Qa→b = Kab(Ta−Tb)

Specific enthalpy hm [J/kg] is distinct from the external convection coefficient h [W/m² K]. Thermal contact resistance r″th [m² K/W] is added at finite-electrode/heater interfaces and is zero within a material. Gas/solid and gas/wall interfaces use the two half-cell conductive resistances. Direct heater/3D-wall contact also uses harmonic conductive coupling; a separate thermal contact resistance for this interface is unavailable.

4.1 Property tables and valid ranges

a(T) = ai + (ai+1−ai)(T−Ti)/(Ti+1−Ti)
ρe(T) = ρe,25[1+α(T−298.15 K)]   (empty heater resistivity table)

Heater and electrode resistivity, conductivity and specific heat each accept an independent temperature table. Tables require at least two points, strictly increasing temperatures and positive property values. A populated table overrides its scalar input. Evaluations outside the tabulated temperature range stop the solve instead of extrapolating. Electrode resistivity is constant when its table is empty. Density, emissivity, gas properties and wall properties are currently constant. Presets are example values; they do not establish properties for a particular grade, porosity, reduction state or temperature range.

Material properties, temperature model & provenance

Source values are at the listed reference temperature. Ω cm × 0.01 = Ω m. Linear models referenced to 20 °C are transformed to the solver’s 25 °C scalar convention without changing their temperature dependence. Catalog Jmax values are retained for traceability and are never applied automatically. Emissivity and J trip remain user inputs. Manufacturer-checked data and repository-transcribed data are identified separately. Constant and linear proxies have no experimentally established temperature range here.

4.2 Specific heat and stored energy

ΔUa = ρm,aVc ∫ToldTnew Cp,a(T)dT
∫T1T2 [C0+b(T−T0)]dT = C0(T2−T1) + b[(T2−T0)²−(T1−T0)²]/2

The enthalpy integral is split at each crossed table knot. Stored heat therefore uses the piecewise-linear Cp integral rather than Cp(Tnew) multiplied by ΔT. Iterations use a secant heat capacity derived from this integral; for a very small temperature change, the local Cp is used. Gas and wall heat storage use ρCpVΔT because their specific heats are constant.

4.3 External boundaries without resolved gas flow

2k/Δℓ · (Tcell−Ts) = h(Ts−Tamb) + εσSB(Ts⁴−Tamb⁴)
hrad = εσSB(Ts+Tamb)(Ts²+Tamb²)
Kboundary = A/[Δℓ/(2k) + 1/(h+hrad)]
σSB = 5.670374419×10−8 W m−2 K−4

A Newton iteration obtains surface temperature Ts and couples half-cell conduction to external convection and radiation. Terminal patches use their own sink temperature and conductance hc; convection and radiation are not added again at those faces. In ideal-terminal mode, additional thermal contact resistance is in series with 1/hc. With gas flow enabled and the reactor wall disabled, external h and heater radiation are disabled: gas conduction, advection and fixed-temperature channel walls remove heat.

5. Reactor walls, insulation and radiation

5.1 Volumetric wall cells

Cylinder, rectangular shell, CSG and closed-STL modes solve an independent temperature in every wall cell. A cylinder uses Channel width as its inner diameter and adds twice the wall thickness to obtain the outer diameter. A rectangular shell uses Channel width and height as its internal dimensions. CAD/STL walls are explicit solids; changing the thickness scalar does not offset their surfaces.

ρwCp,w ∂Tw/∂t = ∇·(kw∇Tw)
Kw,ab = kwAf/Δℓ
Uw(t)−Uw(0) = Σwall cells ρwCp,wVc[Tw(t)−Tw(0)]

Wall cells are excluded from the gas velocity domain. Empty space connected to the lateral outer boundary is classified as exterior. Internal gas components must connect to both Z ends. Side ports, leaks through an unresolved wall and wall/heater volume overlaps are unsupported or cause an error. Airflow and temperature outside the wall are not solved. Curved boundaries use stair-step cell faces, introducing mesh error in area, effective thickness and heat flux.

5.2 External wall boundaries and insulation

R″out = Δℓ/(2kw) + δins/kins
(Twall cell−Ts)/R″out = h(Ts−Tamb) + εwσSB(Ts⁴−Tamb⁴)

The same external boundary applies to outer wall faces and exposed end rims. With insulation present, Ts is the temperature at its outer surface. Insulation adds resistance without adding cells or thermal storage. Each face uses the planar δ/k resistance rather than the exact logarithmic radial resistance of cylindrical insulation. The 3D view reports wall cell-center temperature, not the external surface temperature.

5.3 Internal radiation approximation

εpair = [1/εheater + 1/εwall − 1]−1
Qa→b = εpairσSBAa(Ta⁴−Tb⁴)
Krad,ab = εpairσSBAa(Ta+Tb)(Ta²+Tb²)

One normal ray leaves each exposed nonterminal heater face. After crossing gas cells, it couples to the first wall cell encountered. The same heat rate is removed from the heater and added to the wall, preserving internal energy exchange. A ray that misses the wall contributes no radiation transfer. A ray intercepted by another heater face does not establish a wall connection. Iterations use the previous estimate of the receiving cell temperature.

This is a local gray-surface exchange approximation. It does not solve angular view factors, multiple reflections, wall-to-wall radiation, wavelength dependence, gas absorption/emission or quartz transmission. The effective emissivity expression does not constitute a general radiosity solution for arbitrary curvature or area ratios. Ray coverage reports the emitting face-area fraction whose rays reach a wall; it is not a physical view factor. The quartz preset is treated as an opaque gray material.

5.4 Legacy axial shell

A legacy mode retains a circumferentially uniform wall for older settings. Each Z interval has two cells through its thickness, with radial interlayer conduction, axial conduction and thermal storage. Wall volume is approximated as internal rectangular perimeter × length × thickness. Wall ends are adiabatic. Heater faces couple radiatively to the inner wall cell at the same Z interval. Both reported wall temperatures are through-thickness cell-center values. Geometry and end boundaries differ from the volumetric wall modes, so their results need not coincide.

6. Gas velocity and pressure: Stokes flow

∇·u = 0
−∇p + μ∇²u = 0

The velocity model is steady incompressible Stokes flow with constant density and viscosity. It omits the inertial term ρ(u·∇)u, transient momentum, buoyancy and turbulence. Quantitative use requires conditions where inertia is negligible, typically Reynolds number much smaller than one. A converged result at higher Re does not establish the validity of neglecting inertia. Thermal expansion and low-Mach density/velocity coupling are also omitted.

6.1 Variable placement and boundaries

For the automatically resolved thin-plate mesh, an axially invariant gas cross-section uses the fully developed Stokes solution: transverse velocity is zero, pressure varies linearly along Z, and axial velocity solves μ∇²⊥u_z = −Δp/L with no-slip boundaries. This reduction is used only after checking that gas occupancy is identical in every axial slice. Gas energy remains a three-dimensional conduction/advection solve. For varying cross-sections, the full momentum/continuity solve remains active. A staggered MAC arrangement places pressure at cell centers and each velocity component on its corresponding cell faces. Solid and reactor-wall surfaces impose no slip. The −Z inlet and +Z outlet use pressure/traction boundaries; the viscous and pressure solves determine the velocity profile. Viscous operators use a normal zero-gradient treatment at open axial ends. A uniform inlet velocity profile is not prescribed.

Kuu = DTp + f
D u = 0
D Ku−1DTp = −D Ku−1f

D sums outward volumetric face fluxes in each cell, and Ku is the viscous momentum matrix. The solver first computes velocity and pressure for a unit pressure drop, then scales the fields to the requested total volumetric flow. Consequently, changing flow rate in Screening scales the current Stokes solution without producing inertial changes in flow pattern.

Qunit = Σinlet uf,unitAf
s = Qtarget/Qunit
u = s uunit,   p = s punit,   Δp = s × 1 Pa

Every connected gas component must reach both inlet and outlet. Isolated sealed cavities cause an error because no pressure reference is assigned to them. The bore and exterior of a hollow heater tube can both carry flow when both connect to the ends. Their flow split follows their resistances under the shared pressure drop. The specified flow rate is the sum across all such paths.

6.2 Flow units and Reynolds number

Q [m³/s] = Qactual [mL/min] / (6×107)
Qactual = Qstandard × (Tin/273.15 K) × (101325 Pa/pabs)
Q = ṁ/ρg   (mass-flow input)
Umean = Q/Ain,open
Re = ρgUmeanDh/μ

Standard flow refers to 0 °C and 101325 Pa. Input gas density is not automatically updated using an equation of state. Outlet absolute pressure is used only for standard-to-actual flow conversion, not for a compressible pressure solution. Volumetric wall modes compute hydraulic diameter as Dh = 4A/P from open inlet area and its grid-resolved wetted perimeter. The legacy rectangular channel uses Dh = 2WH/(W+H). Local Reynolds numbers in narrow gaps can differ from the reported value.

7. Gas energy and conjugate heat transfer

ρgCp,g ∂Tg/∂t + ρgCp,gu·∇Tg = ∇·(kg∇Tg)
Ff = ρgCp,gufAf   [W/K]
Qadv,f = FfTupwind

First-order upwind temperature determines advected face enthalpy. Heat leaves the upstream cell and enters the downstream cell with equal magnitude. Numerical diffusion can broaden temperature transitions; outlet temperatures and local gradients require mesh-convergence checks. Gas conduction and solid/gas interface conduction enter the same global thermal system. No additional empirical particle-to-gas heat-transfer coefficient is applied.

The −Z inlet receives enthalpy at Tin and a half-cell conductive temperature boundary. Outlet conduction at +Z is zero. Any incoming advective flux at an open face uses Tin; outgoing flux uses the adjacent gas-cell temperature. With no reactor wall, channel sidewalls are held at Tamb. With a volumetric wall, gas/wall conduction couples to the calculated wall temperature.

The velocity field remains fixed throughout a thermal transient. The gas energy model omits reaction heat, viscous dissipation, pressure work, composition changes and gas radiation. No species conservation equations are present, so gas conversion and selectivity are not predicted.

8. Steady state, time integration and pulses

[Ua(Tn+1)−Ua(Tn)]/Δt + ΣQa→bn+1 + Qboundary,an+1 = PJ,an+1

Transient integration uses first-order backward Euler. Steady studies set the storage term to zero. Heater, electrode and wall initial temperatures use Initial temperature; gas starts at Gas inlet temperature. Steady iterations start from ambient temperature in solids and inlet temperature in gas. Transient studies can instead use a saved completed field. Use last completed field copies the full solid, gas and wall temperatures into the transient initial condition. A changed geometry or domain layout rejects that field. Settings JSON includes the selected initial field. Initial voltage for a slew-limited restart remains zero; supply circuit state is not restored.

on(t) = 1 if (t mod τpulse) < d τpulse, otherwise 0
Δtused = min(Δtinput, tend−t, time to next pulse transition)

Duty d = 1 gives continuous heating and d = 0 gives unpowered cooling. During pulse-off intervals the requested voltage is zero, but a finite slew rate may retain power during the downward ramp. Time intervals end at pulse transitions so they do not skip a switching event. Calculations exceeding 1,200 time intervals stop. Reduce the time step to check convergence of peak temperature, outlet temperature and integrated electrical energy.

History values of P, V and I describe the state used to advance their time interval. At a pulse boundary, they may differ from the mathematical value immediately after switching. Reported heating rate is the final interval's change in average solid temperature divided by its duration; it is zero for steady studies. The history maximum includes both heater and finite electrodes.

9. Nonlinear iteration and linear solvers

  1. Classify geometry, terminals and materials on the grid. If enabled, solve the steady Stokes velocity field.
  2. Evaluate resistivity and conductivity at the estimated temperature. Solve unit potential and apply supply caps, slew and protection conditions.
  3. Assemble conservative electrical heating, internal conduction, advection, boundary losses and thermal storage.
  4. Solve for a new temperature T* and under-relax using T ← T + clamp[0.3(T*−T), −100 K, +100 K]. The step bound prevents a cold-start radiation linearization from overshooting; convergence still uses the unbounded residual.
  5. Require the maximum unrelaxed change max|T*−T| to be below 2×10−6 K. Failure to converge within 140 iterations returns an error.
  6. Reevaluate electrical quantities, boundary heat rates and conservation residuals at the converged temperature, then advance to the next interval.

11. Screening, contours and figure export

xi = xmin + i(xmax−xmin)/(Nx−1)   (linear)
xi = exp[ln xmin + i(ln xmax−ln xmin)/(Nx−1)]   (log)
Nsolves = NxNy

Run captures Model geometry, property tables, supply mode, wall/gas configuration and time settings. Each grid point changes two scalar inputs in that captured copy and runs the same 3D solver again. Later Model edits do not change the captured sweep. Duplicate axis variables, inactive settings and scalar properties overridden by temperature tables are rejected. Sweeping an entire property table by a multiplier is unavailable.

Each axis supports 2–30 points, with at most 400 combinations. Points run sequentially. The worker reuses the last grid when its geometry key matches and reuses constant-property Stokes velocity when geometry, viscosity, gas density and actual volumetric flow match. Thermal coefficients and the electrical solution are recalculated. A selected saved initial field is retained for each transient point; geometry-changing points reject it. Remaining time is estimated from the average elapsed time of completed points. Geometry-dependent refinement and changing nonlinear iteration counts can change the estimate.

Successful points retain scalar outputs such as temperatures, power, pressure drop, residuals and mesh counts. Changing the output redraws stored values without another PDE solve. Sweep files do not retain full 3D fields. Load a selected point into Model and calculate to recover its spatial fields.

Filled contours require four valid corner results. Each grid quadrilateral is split along a consistent diagonal into two triangles for linear interpolation. Logarithmic axes interpolate in log coordinates. Contour lines represent interpolated responses rather than additional PDE solutions. Cells touching a failed, pending or unavailable result remain unfilled. Denser sweeps are required to resolve design boundaries. Extending a displayed log axis to decade boundaries does not extend the calculated range or extrapolate its values.

Black squares mark points whose final supply state differs from Setpoint; red crosses mark failed points. This does not indicate whether a limit was active earlier in a pulse. Stopping discards the unfinished point and retains completed ones. Resume runs pending points with the captured inputs. Each completed point updates a device checkpoint, with storage failures reported in the UI. JSON includes inputs, axes, results and errors; CSV includes per-point scalar outputs.

Temperature histories and V–I plots allocate a 5 × 5 in panel plus separate legend space. Screening allocates a 5 × 5 in panel plus colorbar space. Primary plotting boxes are square, with Arial (Helvetica/DejaVu Sans fallback), white backgrounds, four spines, inward ticks and no grid. Ordered colors run from light to dark through viridis. The 3D physical-domain view retains geometric proportions and is exempt from the square-axis requirement.

SVG export retains text as text. PNG export uses 600 pixels per inch and writes corresponding resolution metadata. Exports check panel allocation, an axes width/height ratio within 1%, and text bounds/overlap before saving. The export status reports measured dimensions. These figure checks are independent of solver verification.

Annular grid, restart and reuse

Select Annular r / θ / z for a hollow solid tube with gas and wall disabled. The bore must be positive. Radial cells span the physical wall thickness; angular cells are periodic. The solver retains the same temperature-dependent electrical feedback, supply caps, contact heat and transient enthalpy equations. Angular and radial cell counts are independent of Z. Limits are 2–64 radial, 8–192 angular and 3–192 axial cells, with at most 60,000 cells.

ΔV = ½(r₊² − r₋²) Δθ Δz
Ar = rf Δθ Δz
Gr = Δθ Δz / [ln(rf/ra)/ka + ln(rb/rf)/kb]
Gθ = Δz ln(r₊/r₋) / [½Δθ/ka + ½Δθ/kb]

The same metric resistances use electrical resistivity for current conduction. Contact resistance adds its area-specific term at material interfaces. Exact volumes and curved areas are used in the operator; displayed surfaces remain polygonal. Thin plate grid sets Cartesian X/Y/Z counts to 16/6/60 for a rectangular solid without gas or wall. It preserves dimensions. Represented solid bounds and cell spacing are shown; this preset does not establish grid convergence.

Verification recovers annular axial resistance to a relative error below 10−7 and the logarithmic radial conduction solution to below 10−5 K on the tested grids. The radial test applies prescribed inner and outer temperatures directly to the production mesh operator; these are test boundaries, not additional UI options. The Wismann-like ambient-loss test changes maximum temperature by approximately 0.022 K between 3 and 6 radial cells at fixed 16 angular and 12 axial cells. This is a radial refinement check, not a full experimental validation or axial convergence study.

Use last completed field captures a full temperature array in kelvin. Changing geometry, electrodes, grid counts or domain selection requires a compatible new field. A transient restart begins its energy ledger at the captured temperature. Export settings preserves this array and benchmark metadata; importing restores both. Cached geometry and velocity are held only in the current worker. Cancellation releases that worker. Gas temperature, solid temperature and pressure boundary labels are rebuilt from the current inputs; thermal fields are not implicitly reused as initial conditions.

12. Verification, literature benchmarks and omissions

Included numerical checks cover uniform-bar resistance and contact heat, asymmetric terminal currents, parabolic-temperature mesh convergence, pulse energy conservation, supply caps/slew/trips, square-duct Stokes flow, solid/gas energy conservation, CSG/STL classification, finite-electrode heat storage, Cp knot integration, analytical k(T) conduction, 3D wall conduction/storage/circumferential variation, cylindrical-wall logarithmic temperatures and hollow-tube connectivity. Screening checks cover small actual solver sweeps, axis sampling, invalid-region masking and preservation of captured inputs.

The Wismann grid pair tests transverse representation only; it does not establish full mesh convergence. Circular boundaries use stair-step cells, which alters effective area. Zheng electrical discrepancies include this area error and the constant-resistivity assumption. Small conservation residuals do not resolve these uncertainties. The nonlinear update is bounded to 100 K per iteration to prevent cold-start radiation overshoot; the original unbounded convergence residual and temperature range checks remain enforced.

A resistance fit used as an input cannot independently validate that same resistance output. Power calculated as V×I is not an independent measurement of V and I. Applying porosity again to properties that already represent an effective porous medium can count its influence twice. Presets do not automatically detect this.

Omitted capabilities include inertial Navier–Stokes flow, turbulence, low-Mach thermal expansion, temperature-dependent gas properties/composition, reactions and species transport, phase changes, stress and thermal deformation, anisotropic conduction, independent supplies, general electrode CAD contact-pair assignment, STEP/BREP geometry, full angular radiosity, quartz transmission, physical supply-circuit dynamics, automatic inverse design and adaptive meshing. Volumetric walls currently use constant properties. Annular coordinates currently support a solid hollow tube with ideal terminals or finite end electrodes and ambient losses; gas and reactor-wall coupling remain Cartesian.
