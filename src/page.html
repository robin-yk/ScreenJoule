Gas flow & heat transfer

Constant-property Stokes flow; inertia and turbulence omitted. Intended for Re ≪ 1. With the wall enabled, wall heat storage and enclosure radiation are coupled to gas/solid heat transfer. Otherwise channel walls are fixed at ambient. Density is a user input; absolute pressure is used only for standard-flow conversion.

Reactor wall
STL units: mm. The bore must remain open at both Z ends. Import recenters the wall and sets total length.

Cylinder: channel width is inner diameter. Box: channel width/height are inner dimensions. CAD/STL use explicit wall solids; thickness input does not reshape them. Every wall voxel has an independent temperature. Outer surfaces and end rims exchange heat with ambient. Refine until thickness is resolved. Quartz is treated as opaque; radiation uses face-normal rays, without angular view factors or transmission.

Finite end electrodes

Each electrode replaces a length at one end of the total solid envelope. Zero retains ideal end terminals. Heater length equals total length minus both electrodes. Geometry follows the solid cross-section.

Illustrative copper constants. Contact resistances move to electrode–heater interfaces; interface heat is divided equally between adjacent cells. Material boundaries follow voxel faces.

Contact & protection

With ideal terminals, contact resistance applies to both end patches and contact heat enters the adjacent heater cell. With finite electrodes enabled, their voltage, temperature, conduction and heat storage are solved.

Mesh & target window

Independent cell counts. Zero uses the mesh preset. Hollow tubes enforce finer X/Y spacing to resolve the wall and bore; the actual grid is shown below the view. Maximum 60,000 cells; uniform Cartesian spacing.

Temperature-dependent material tables

A filled table overrides that scalar property. Use one temperature/property pair per line, with increasing temperatures. Enter source and temperature range below.

Piecewise-linear interpolation; no extrapolation. Stored solid heat uses density × volume × ∫Cp(T)dT. Empty tables retain scalar inputs. Selecting a heater preset replaces its three tables with that preset’s temperature model.
